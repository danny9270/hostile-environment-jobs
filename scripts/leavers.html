Timeline

Resettlement can start up to two years before you leave. The people who walk into good first contracts usually started early.

  1. 2 years out
    • Book your first interview with your unit resettlement officer and get registered with the Career Transition Partnership (CTP).
    • Check you're registered for Enhanced Learning Credits. If you never opted in, you can't claim them, so check now.
    • Read Getting in and pick one or two lines of work to aim at.
  2. 18 months
    • Do the CTP Career Transition Workshop.
    • Plan which courses you'll fund with ELC, IRTC and SLC, and in which financial year (one ELC claim per year).
    • Get a pension forecast so you know what you'll be living on.
  3. 12 months
    • Start the courses that take longest or need booking early: close protection, FREC, NEBOSH, IMAS EOD.
    • Build your CV in civilian language with the CV builder and get it in front of people who've done the job.
    • Set up job alerts so you see what's being advertised and what it pays.
  4. 6 months
    • Renew your passport if it has under two years left or few blank pages, and get your vaccinations and medicals sorted.
    • Use resettlement time (GRT) for interviews, attachments or a CTP job finding course.
    • Start applying. Many contracts recruit three to four months ahead of the start date.
  5. Last 3 months
    • Collect your service records, course certificates and references. They're much harder to get once you're out.
    • Sort GP registration, banking and tax residence plans if you'll be working abroad.
    • Keep three months of money in hand. First contracts slip, and pay is often a month in arrears.

Resettlement funding

UK forces leavers can usually stack several of these. Spend the money on courses that employers actually ask for in adverts, not just the ones that are easy to book.

Enhanced Learning Credits

ELC · through ELCAS

Up to £2,000 a year
  • Lower tier up to £1,000 a claim, higher tier up to £2,000 (usually 8+ years' service).
  • Up to three claims, one per financial year. You pay at least 20% of the fee.
  • Course must be Level 3 or above, with an ELCAS approved provider.
  • You must have registered for ELC while serving. Claims can be made for several years after leaving; check your own cut-off.

ELCAS website and provider search

IRTC grant

Individual Resettlement Training Costs

Up to £534
  • For leavers with 6+ years' service, and all medical discharges.
  • Pays tuition on CTP courses or with CTP preferred suppliers. Exam and registration fees are extra.
  • Can often be combined with ELC on the same course; ask your resettlement officer how.

CTP allowances and grants

Standard Learning Credits

SLC · education centre

Up to £175 a year
  • Pays 80% of small courses, exams and books, every financial year while serving and in resettlement.
  • Good for first aid tickets, online modules, licence fees and exam resits.
  • Approval must come before the course starts.

Standard Learning Credits

Resettlement time

GRT · Graduated Resettlement Time

Up to 35 working days
  • Paid time off for courses, attachments, workshops and interviews, from up to two years before you leave.
  • The days you get depend on service length (6+ years or a medical discharge for the full allowance).
  • Travel for approved resettlement activity can usually be claimed too.

CTP on GOV.UK

Less than 6 years in, or leaving early? You still get CTP help (the Employment Support Programme or Future Horizons for early leavers) and can use SLC. The Forces Employment Charity helps for life, whenever you left.

Which courses count

ELC needs a Level 3 or higher course from an ELCAS approved provider. Plenty of what the circuit asks for qualifies, but not all of it. Always ask the provider for their ELCAS number before you book, and search for it on the ELCAS site.

Before you sign your first contract

A first contract is often lower paid than the ones after it, and that's fine. What matters is that it's safe, it pays when it says it will, and you know what you've agreed to. Tick these off as you go; we keep your ticks in this browser only.

  • Day rate or salary, and when it's paid. Is it paid on leave days? Monthly in arrears? In which currency? Compare it with the pay guide.
  • Rotation and travel. The rotation in writing (for example 8 on, 4 off), who books and pays flights, and whether travel days are paid.
  • Insurance. Medical and medevac cover in a hostile area, life and disability cover that doesn't exclude war risks, and whether you're covered on leave. If you're self employed, you may need to buy your own.
  • Employed, self employed or through your own company. This changes your tax, insurance and notice. Get advice before setting up a limited company just because an agency asks.
  • Tax. Working abroad doesn't automatically mean tax free. UK residence depends on the Statutory Residence Test (days in the UK, home, work). Think about voluntary National Insurance to protect your state pension.
  • Notice and termination. How much notice each side gives, what happens if the contract ends early, and whether you're paid if you're sent home for security reasons.
  • Who pays for what. HEAT, medicals, visas, vaccinations, kit and licences. Many employers pay; some take it out of your first month.
  • Documents ready. Passport with 6+ months left and blank pages (a second passport helps for some regions), driving licence and international permit, certificates, references, DBS or vetting if asked.
  • The company is real. Look for a registered company, a working website and people on LinkedIn who've worked there. Read the deployment guide for the country.
  • You're not paying for the job. Never pay a recruiter or employer to be hired or to "secure" a visa. That's a scam, every time.

Who can help

Free help for UK service leavers and veterans. They don't charge you, and most help however long ago you left.

Career Transition Partnership

The MOD's official resettlement service: workshops, courses, job finding and the employer network.

ctp.org.uk

Forces Employment Charity

Free employment advice and job matching for life, for veterans, reservists and families.

forcesemployment.org.uk

Officers' Association

Career help for officers leaving the forces, including management and senior roles.

officersassociation.org.uk

X-Forces Enterprise

Help if you want to set up your own business, such as a training or consultancy company.

x-forces.com

Felix Fund

The bomb disposal charity, supporting serving and former EOD and search personnel and their families.

felixfund.org.uk

Op COURAGE

The NHS mental health service for veterans and service leavers in England. Similar services run in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.

Op COURAGE on GOV.UK

Not leaving the UK forces? US service members can look at DoD SkillBridge, and Australian and Canadian forces have their own transition services. Most of the first contract checklist above applies wherever you served.
