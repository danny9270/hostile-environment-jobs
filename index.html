Humanitarian mine action and EOD

Most people come from military EOD or combat engineer roles, but national staff and career changers start as deminers and work up.

  • Start as a deminer or EOD operator; IMAS EOD Level 1 and 2 are the entry qualifications, and many NGOs train national staff themselves.
  • Move to IMAS EOD Level 3 to lead teams, then Level 3+ or specialist courses (BAC, ammunition, PMM) for senior technical roles.
  • Non-technical survey, EORE and community liaison roles suit people without an ammunition background.
  • Expect HEAT and a first aid ticket to be asked for on almost every application.

IEDD and commercial EOD

These roles nearly always go to people with military or police IEDD qualifications and operational experience.

  • UK, US and NATO high-threat or IEDD operator courses are the usual baseline; civilian equivalents are rarer and checked carefully.
  • Commercial UXO survey and clearance (offshore wind, construction) is a common route into the civilian world.
  • Instructor and mentor roles with partner forces suit operators with teaching experience.

Military search

Search advisers usually come from military search teams; trained searchers can also move into mine action survey work.

  • Search team member, then search team commander, then search adviser qualifications are the usual ladder.
  • Training and mentoring national search teams is where most civilian search jobs are.
  • Pair search experience with an IMAS qualification to widen your options.

Security

Covers close protection, PSD, site security and NGO security management. Each needs different credentials.

  • Close protection: an SIA CP licence (UK) or local equivalent, a recognised CP course and an emergency first aid qualification.
  • NGO security management: experience matters more than tickets, but courses in security risk management and HEAT help you get shortlisted.
  • Former military and police have a head start; analysts and coordinators can come from humanitarian or political backgrounds.
  • Check each country's licensing rules before you take a contract.

Hostile environment medic

Employers want proven trauma care plus the ability to work alone, far from a hospital.

  • Paramedics, nurses and military medics are the main routes; FREC 4 or 5 opens doors to remote medic roles if you're not registered.
  • Add a pre-hospital trauma course (PHTLS, TCCC or similar) and remote or tactical medicine training.
  • Many medic roles also cover HSE, so a NEBOSH or similar safety certificate is useful.

Support, logistics and management

Every field programme needs people for logistics, finance, HR, data, fleet and management, and many need no ammunition background.

  • Transfer your skills from the military, NGOs or business, and highlight work in tough places.
  • IMSMA, GIS and data skills are in short supply in mine action.
  • Programme managers usually need donor reporting, budget and people management experience.

About

Hostile Environment Jobs grew out of our Facebook group, where people working in EOD, search and mine action share vacancies. It now covers everyone who works in hard places: security, medics, trainers and support staff too. This site keeps every open job in one place, searchable, with closed jobs removed automatically.

Privacy

We don't use cookies or track you across sites. We count page views with GoatCounter, which stores no cookies and no personal data, so we can see which jobs people look at. If you send a job, we use your name and email only to reply about that job. If you review a course, we keep it on our own server until we've checked it, show only the name and background you choose, and use your email only to check the review with you. Job forms are delivered by FormSubmit. If you subscribe to the Sitrep, we keep your email address with our newsletter provider, Kit, only to send it; every email has an unsubscribe link. When you click through to book a course, the link tells the provider you came from us so they can credit us; it carries no personal data. If you join the talent pool, we keep your profile, contact details and CV on our own server (a Cloudflare Worker and database). Employers we've checked and who pay for search see your profile without your name, contact details or CV; those go to an employer only when you accept their request, and that employer is then responsible for how they use them. Your profile leaves search 90 days after you last confirm you're available and is deleted, with your CV, after 12 months, or straight away when you delete it yourself. If you sign up to search as an employer, we keep your company, name and email to run your account. We use your email only to tell you about requests and approvals. Ask us any time to delete what you've sent.
